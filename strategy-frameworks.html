Business Strategy Frameworks

Strategic frameworks provide structured approaches to analyze businesses, industries, and competitive dynamics. Consultants and corporate strategists use these tools to diagnose problems, identify opportunities, and develop recommendations. Master these frameworks to think strategically about business challenges.

Porter's Five Forces

Purpose: Analyze industry attractiveness and competitive intensity

Created by: Michael Porter, Harvard Business School (1979)

Threat of New Entrants

Barriers to entry, capital requirements, economies of scale

Supplier Power

Supplier concentration, switching costs, forward integration

COMPETITIVE RIVALRY
Buyer Power

Buyer concentration, price sensitivity, backward integration

Threat of Substitutes

Alternative products, relative price-performance, switching ease

How to Use:

  • Analyze each force: Rate as high, medium, or low
  • Stronger forces = less attractive industry: High competitive intensity reduces profitability
  • Identify strategic opportunities: Can you reduce force intensity? (build barriers, lock in suppliers, differentiate from substitutes)

Example Application

Airline Industry: High rivalry (many competitors), high buyer power (price-sensitive customers), high threat of new entrants (when regulations allow), moderate supplier power (Boeing/Airbus duopoly), low substitute threat (limited alternatives for long distance). Result: Structurally unattractive industry with low margins.

SWOT Analysis

Purpose: Assess strategic position and identify action items

Strengths (Internal)

What advantages do we have? Core competencies, resources, capabilities

Weaknesses (Internal)

What disadvantages? Gaps in capabilities, resource constraints, vulnerabilities

Opportunities (External)

Market trends, unmet needs, regulatory changes, new technologies

Threats (External)

Competition, market shifts, economic factors, disruptive technologies

Strategic Actions:

  • Leverage Strengths to Capture Opportunities: What can we uniquely do?
  • Shore Up Weaknesses: What gaps must we fill to compete?
  • Mitigate Threats: How can we defend against competitive forces?
  • Convert Weaknesses to Strengths: Strategic investments or partnerships

BCG Growth-Share Matrix

Purpose: Portfolio analysis for multi-product/business companies

Created by: Boston Consulting Group (1970)

⭐ Stars

High Growth, High Market Share

Strategy: Invest to maintain leadership

❓ Question Marks

High Growth, Low Market Share

Strategy: Invest heavily or divest

💰 Cash Cows

Low Growth, High Market Share

Strategy: Harvest cash to fund Stars

🐕 Dogs

Low Growth, Low Market Share

Strategy: Divest or minimize investment

Portfolio Strategy: Use cash from Cash Cows to invest in Stars and promising Question Marks. Exit Dogs. Ideal portfolio has a balance of cash generators and growth opportunities.

Value Chain Analysis

Purpose: Identify sources of competitive advantage in operations

Created by: Michael Porter (1985)

Primary Activities (Direct value creation):

  • Inbound Logistics: Receiving, warehousing, inventory management
  • Operations: Manufacturing, assembly, quality control
  • Outbound Logistics: Distribution, order fulfillment, delivery
  • Marketing & Sales: Advertising, pricing, channel management
  • Service: Installation, training, repair, customer support

Support Activities (Enable primary activities):

  • Firm Infrastructure: Finance, legal, quality management
  • Human Resource Management: Recruiting, training, compensation
  • Technology Development: R&D, process automation, product design
  • Procurement: Purchasing raw materials, negotiating with vendors

Application

Analyze each activity to find where you create the most value and where you can improve efficiency. Example: Amazon excels at outbound logistics (fast delivery) and technology (recommendation algorithms), creating competitive advantage.

Ansoff Growth Matrix

Purpose: Identify growth strategies based on products and markets

Market Penetration

Existing Product, Existing Market

Lowest risk: Increase market share through pricing, promotion, distribution

Market Development

Existing Product, New Market

Medium risk: Enter new geographies, segments, or channels

Product Development

New Product, Existing Market

Medium risk: Create new products for current customers

Diversification

New Product, New Market

Highest risk: Enter entirely new businesses

The 4Ps Marketing Mix

Purpose: Design comprehensive marketing strategy

  • Product: Features, quality, design, branding, packaging, warranties
    • What needs does it satisfy?
    • How does it differentiate from competitors?
  • Price: Pricing strategy, discounts, payment terms, perceived value
    • Premium pricing vs. cost leadership?
    • How price-sensitive are customers?
  • Place (Distribution): Channels, locations, inventory, logistics
    • Where do customers buy?
    • Direct sales vs. intermediaries?
  • Promotion: Advertising, PR, sales force, digital marketing
    • What message resonates with target customers?
    • Which channels reach them effectively?

PESTEL Analysis

Purpose: Analyze macro-environmental factors affecting business

  • Political: Government stability, tax policy, trade regulations, labor laws
  • Economic: GDP growth, interest rates, inflation, unemployment, exchange rates
  • Social: Demographics, cultural trends, lifestyle changes, education levels
  • Technological: Innovation, automation, R&D activity, tech adoption rates
  • Environmental: Climate change, sustainability, resource scarcity, carbon regulations
  • Legal: Consumer protection, antitrust, IP rights, employment law, safety regulations

Use Case: Understanding external forces when entering new markets or planning long-term strategy.

When to Use Each Framework

Framework Best For
Porter's Five Forces Industry analysis, market entry decisions
SWOT Strategic planning, business assessment
BCG Matrix Portfolio management, resource allocation
Value Chain Operational efficiency, cost reduction
Ansoff Matrix Growth strategy, expansion planning
4Ps Marketing strategy, product launches
PESTEL Macro-environmental scanning, scenario planning

Framework Selection Tip

Don't force-fit frameworks. Choose based on the question you're trying to answer. Often, you'll combine multiple frameworks (e.g., PESTEL for external environment + SWOT for company position + Porter's for industry dynamics) for comprehensive analysis.
