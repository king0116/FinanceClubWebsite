Consulting Case Interview Preparation

Case interviews are the primary method consulting firms use to evaluate candidates. They test your ability to solve business problems in real-time using structured thinking, quantitative analysis, and clear communication. This guide covers everything you need to know to excel.

The Case Interview Structure

Mastering the case interview requires understanding the four-step process consultants use to solve problems:

1

Clarify

Understand the problem, ask clarifying questions, confirm objectives

2

Structure

Create a framework to organize your analysis

3

Analyze

Work through your framework, perform calculations, test hypotheses

4

Synthesize

Deliver a clear recommendation with supporting rationale

Key Case Types

1. Market Sizing Cases

Estimate the size of a market or specific metric using logical assumptions and mathematical reasoning.

Example: "How many gas stations are in the United States?"

Approaches:

  • Top-Down: Start with total population (330M Americans) → households (130M) → cars per household (1.5) → cars (195M) → gas stations needed based on cars per station
  • Bottom-Up: Start with one gas station → customers per day → scale up based on population density and geography

Key Skills: Making reasonable assumptions, breaking problems into components, mental math, sense-checking answers

2. Profitability Cases

Diagnose why a company's profits are declining or identify opportunities to improve profitability.

Framework: Profit = Revenue - Costs

Revenue Drivers:

  • Price: Pricing strategy, discounts, product mix
  • Quantity: Sales volume, market share, customer acquisition/retention

Cost Drivers:

  • Fixed Costs: Rent, salaries, overhead, depreciation
  • Variable Costs: Cost of goods sold (COGS), direct labor, commissions

Common Issues: Increased competition driving down prices, rising input costs, operational inefficiencies, shifts in product mix toward lower-margin items

3. Market Entry Cases

Determine whether a company should enter a new market, launch a new product, or pursue an acquisition.

Framework: Market Attractiveness + Company Fit

Market Attractiveness:

  • Market size and growth rate
  • Competitive intensity (Porter's Five Forces)
  • Profitability and margins
  • Regulatory environment

Company Fit:

  • Core competencies and capabilities
  • Resources required vs. available
  • Strategic alignment with company goals
  • Competitive advantage / differentiation

4. M&A / Investment Cases

Evaluate whether a company should acquire another company or make a strategic investment.

Key Considerations:

  • Strategic Rationale: Does this acquisition create value? Revenue synergies? Cost synergies?
  • Financial Analysis: Valuation (DCF, comparable companies), ROI, payback period
  • Integration Risk: Cultural fit, technology systems, customer retention
  • Alternatives: Organic growth, partnerships, smaller acquisitions

Mental Math Tips

Quick calculations are essential in case interviews. Here are strategies used by successful candidates:

  • Round strategically: 47 × 23 ≈ 50 × 20 = 1,000 (then adjust: actual is slightly higher, ~1,081)
  • Break down percentages: 15% of 240 = 10% (24) + 5% (12) = 36
  • Use benchmarks: Memorize that 1/7 ≈ 14%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 ≈ 11%
  • Simplify fractions: 450/75 = 45/7.5 = 90/15 = 6

Pro Tip: The 80/20 Rule in Case Interviews

Focus 80% of your preparation time on mastering frameworks and structured thinking. The remaining 20% should go to mental math practice and industry knowledge. Most candidates fail due to poor structure, not calculation errors.

Common Frameworks

The Profitability Framework

Use when a company's profits are declining or you need to improve margins:

  • Revenue = Price × Quantity
  • Costs = Fixed Costs + Variable Costs
  • Profit = Revenue - Costs

The 4Cs Framework

General business analysis framework:

  • Company: Internal capabilities, resources, strengths/weaknesses
  • Customers: Needs, segments, buying behavior, willingness to pay
  • Competition: Direct competitors, substitutes, market share, competitive advantages
  • Context: Industry trends, regulations, economic factors, technology changes

Porter's Five Forces

Analyze industry competitiveness:

  • Threat of new entrants
  • Bargaining power of suppliers
  • Bargaining power of buyers
  • Threat of substitute products
  • Competitive rivalry among existing firms

The STAR Method for Fit Interviews

Consulting interviews also include behavioral questions. Use STAR to structure your answers:

  • Situation: Set the context (what was happening?)
  • Task: Describe your responsibility (what did you need to accomplish?)
  • Action: Explain what you did (be specific about YOUR actions)
  • Result: Share the outcome (quantify when possible: "increased sales by 25%")

Practice Recommendation

Aim for 20-30 practice cases before your interview. Do 60% solo practice (to build frameworks and structure), 30% with partners (to practice verbal communication), and 10% mock interviews with experienced consultants or coaches for feedback.

Sample Case Walkthrough

Case Prompt: "Your client is a national coffee shop chain experiencing declining profits over the past year despite steady revenue. What would you investigate?"

Step 1 - Clarify:

  • "Can you clarify the timeframe - has this been happening for exactly one year or gradually over time?"
  • "Is revenue truly flat, or has it grown slightly while profits declined?"
  • "Are we looking at company-wide profits or specific regions/stores?"

Step 2 - Structure:

"Since revenue is stable but profits are declining, I'd like to use a profitability framework focusing on the cost side. I'll examine both fixed and variable costs, then explore operational factors."

My Structure:

  • Revenue (baseline check)
  • Fixed Costs: Rent, salaries, overhead
  • Variable Costs: Coffee beans, milk, supplies, labor per transaction
  • Operational Factors: Waste, efficiency, product mix

Step 3 - Analyze:

"Let me start by confirming revenue is truly stable..." [Work through each branch of your framework based on data provided]

Step 4 - Synthesize:

"Based on my analysis, I recommend the client focus on three areas: (1) Renegotiate coffee bean supply contracts to reduce the 15% cost increase, (2) Implement waste reduction training to address the 8% spoilage rate, and (3) Adjust staffing models to reduce labor costs during off-peak hours. Together, these should restore profitability to previous levels within 6 months."
