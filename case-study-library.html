Practice with real-world business cases that mirror consulting interviews and strategic challenges. Each case includes the problem statement, suggested framework, analysis approach, and recommended solution. Work through these to build your case-solving skills.

Case 1: Declining Retail Chain Profitability

Profitability Retail Operations

📋 Problem Statement

Your client is a national retail chain with 200 stores across the United States. Over the past two years, overall profitability has declined by 15%, despite revenue remaining relatively stable (growing only 2% annually). The CEO has hired your consulting firm to diagnose the issue and recommend solutions.

Key Data Provided:

  • Revenue: $500M (up 2% from $490M two years ago)
  • Net margin: 3% (down from 7% two years ago)
  • Number of stores: Unchanged at 200
  • Average transaction value: Stable at $45
  • Customer traffic: Down 5% year-over-year

🎯 Recommended Framework

Profitability Framework: Profit = Revenue - Costs

Since revenue is stable but profits are declining, focus on the cost side:

  • Revenue Check: Confirm components (price × quantity) and any mix shifts
  • Fixed Costs: Rent, corporate overhead, store salaries
  • Variable Costs: Cost of goods sold (COGS), shipping, sales commissions
  • Operational Factors: Inventory management, waste/shrinkage, labor efficiency

🔍 Analysis & Findings

Revenue Analysis:

Revenue = Price × Quantity $500M = $45 × 11.1M transactions (Note: Customer traffic down 5%, but average basket size up slightly)

Cost Analysis Reveals:

  • COGS increased 12%: Investigation shows supplier costs rose due to poor contract renegotiation and loss of volume discounts from declining purchases
  • Inventory shrinkage at 8%: Industry average is 2-3%. Root cause: inadequate inventory controls and outdated point-of-sale systems
  • Labor costs up 6%: Due to inefficient scheduling; stores overstaffed during slow periods
  • Distribution costs up 7%: Increased rush shipping to address stockouts from poor inventory forecasting

Impact Calculation:

Baseline Net Margin (2 years ago): 7% of $490M = $34.3M profit Current Net Margin: 3% of $500M = $15M profit Profit Decline: $19.3M (56% reduction) Contributing Factors: - COGS increase: ~$6M impact - Shrinkage (excess 5%): ~$25M impact - Labor inefficiency: ~$3M impact - Rush shipping: ~$3.5M impact

💡 Recommendations

Immediate Actions (0-3 months):

  • Renegotiate supplier contracts: Consolidate vendors, commit to volume guarantees in exchange for 8-10% cost reduction
  • Implement inventory control system: RFID tagging and improved POS integration to reduce shrinkage to 3% within 6 months
  • Optimize labor scheduling: Use data analytics to match staffing to traffic patterns, reducing labor costs by 4%

Medium-term Initiatives (3-12 months):

  • Demand forecasting system: Reduce stockouts and rush shipping through better inventory planning
  • Store productivity review: Identify and close/relocate bottom 10% of stores by profitability
  • Category management: Shift product mix toward higher-margin items

Expected Impact:

Total Potential Margin Improvement: 5-6 percentage points Timeline to 7% margins: 12-18 months Investment Required: $8M (systems + severance) ROI: Positive within 9 months

Case 2: Software Company Market Entry

Market Entry Technology Strategy

📋 Problem Statement

Your client is a successful B2B SaaS company providing project management software to mid-market companies in North America. They are considering entering the European market. The CEO wants to know if this is a good strategic move and, if so, how to approach it.

Client Background:

  • Current revenue: $80M ARR (annual recurring revenue)
  • Growth rate: 30% year-over-year
  • Customer base: 2,000 companies (avg $40K/year subscription)
  • Gross margin: 85%
  • Target market: 50-500 employee companies

🎯 Recommended Framework

Market Entry Framework: Market Attractiveness + Company Fit

Market Attractiveness:

  • Market size and growth
  • Competitive landscape
  • Customer needs and willingness to pay
  • Regulatory environment

Company Fit:

  • Can we compete effectively?
  • Do we have required capabilities?
  • What's the investment required?
  • Strategic alignment with company goals

🔍 Analysis

Market Attractiveness - POSITIVE:

  • Market Size: European project management software market is $2.5B, growing 25% annually
  • Target Segment: ~150,000 mid-market companies in EU (3x North American market)
  • Competitive Gap: European competitors focus on enterprise; mid-market is underserved
  • Regulatory: GDPR compliance required but manageable with existing infrastructure

Company Fit - MIXED:

  • ✓ Product-Market Fit: Customer needs similar to North America
  • ✓ Competitive Advantage: Superior UX and pricing vs. European alternatives
  • ✗ Localization Required: Need to support 5+ languages, local payment methods
  • ✗ Sales Infrastructure: Current remote sales model may not work; Europeans prefer local presence
  • ✗ Customer Support: Need local time zone coverage

Investment Analysis:

Year 1 Investment: - Product localization: $2M - European sales team (10 reps): $1.5M - Marketing/brand building: $1M - Legal/compliance: $500K Total: $5M Revenue Projection: Year 1: $5M ARR (125 customers @ $40K avg) Year 2: $15M ARR (with 200% growth) Year 3: $35M ARR (with 130% growth) Payback Period: ~18 months

💡 Recommendation

Decision: YES, enter the European market

Rationale:

  • Large, fast-growing market with underserved mid-market segment
  • Clear competitive advantage in target segment
  • Reasonable investment with strong ROI potential
  • De-risks company by diversifying geographically

Entry Strategy:

  • Phase 1 (Months 1-6): UK market only (English-speaking, largest market, easiest entry)
  • Phase 2 (Months 6-12): Germany and France (largest non-English markets)
  • Phase 3 (Year 2+): Expand to Benelux, Nordics, Southern Europe

Key Success Factors:

  • Hire experienced European sales leader in Month 1
  • Establish partnerships with local implementation consultants
  • Invest in case studies and testimonials from European customers early
  • Adapt pricing to local market conditions (may need 10-15% discount vs. US)

Case 3: Manufacturing Cost Reduction

Operations Cost Reduction Manufacturing

📋 Problem Statement

Your client manufactures industrial equipment and has seen margins compress due to rising material and labor costs. The CFO wants to identify $10M in annual cost savings without reducing quality or laying off workers. Can you help them find these savings?

Current State:

  • Annual revenue: $200M
  • Operating margin: 8% (target: 13%)
  • Manufacturing costs: $140M (70% of revenue)
  • Three factories producing similar products
  • 1,500 employees total

🎯 Framework

Cost Structure Analysis: Break down $140M manufacturing cost

  • Direct materials: 60% ($84M)
  • Direct labor: 20% ($28M)
  • Manufacturing overhead: 15% ($21M)
  • Quality/rework: 5% ($7M)

🔍 Findings & Recommendations

Opportunity 1: Material Cost Optimization ($4M savings)

  • Purchasing decentralized across three factories with no volume leverage
  • Using 15 different steel suppliers; consolidate to 3 preferred vendors
  • Implement centralized procurement with volume commitments
  • Expected savings: 5% on $84M = $4.2M

Opportunity 2: Process Efficiency ($3M savings)

  • Factory layout analysis shows excessive material movement
  • Workers spend 20% of time retrieving parts due to poor organization
  • Implement lean manufacturing principles and cellular layout
  • Expected savings: 10% labor productivity gain = $2.8M

Opportunity 3: Quality Improvement ($2.5M savings)

  • Current defect rate: 5% (industry benchmark: 2%)
  • Root cause: lack of preventive maintenance on critical equipment
  • Implement predictive maintenance and statistical process control
  • Expected savings: Reduce rework by 60% = $2.5M (investment: $500K)

Opportunity 4: Energy & Overhead ($1.5M savings)

  • Factories run 24/7 but production only 60% of time
  • Consolidate production to 2 factories, use 3rd for overflow only
  • LED lighting retrofit and HVAC optimization
  • Expected savings: $1.5M annually
Total Identified Savings: $11.2M Target: $10M Timeline: 12-18 months for full implementation Investment Required: $3M (equipment, layout changes, systems) Net Benefit Year 1: $8M+

Implementation Priorities:

  • Quick Wins (Months 1-3): Procurement consolidation, energy audit
  • Medium-term (Months 3-9): Process improvements, quality systems
  • Long-term (Months 9-18): Factory consolidation, layout optimization

Practice Recommendations

Work through each case multiple times: (1) First pass - outline your approach without looking at the answer, (2) Second pass - work through the full analysis, (3) Third pass - practice articulating your recommendation out loud as if presenting to a client. This builds both analytical and communication skills.
